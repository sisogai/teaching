2.1-2.3 Goods, Markets, and Agents

An economic agent is any person or entity making a decision. Goods and services are objects of choice and trade, including location-specific, time-specific, and state-contingent goods.

Market

A market is a place, institution, or mechanism through which agents interact and trade a good. Consumers are usually on the demand side of goods markets, while producers are on the supply side.

2.4 Demand, Supply, and Equilibrium

Equilibrium occurs where the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied. If the price is too high, excess supply creates downward pressure; if it is too low, excess demand creates upward pressure.

Model: Linear Demand and Supply

The quantity demanded is \(Q_D(p) = A - Bp\) (downward sloping in price) and the quantity supplied is \(Q_S(p) = C + Dp\) (upward sloping). Setting \(Q_D = Q_S\) gives the market-clearing price: $$ p^* = \frac{A - C}{B + D}, \qquad Q^* = A - B p^*. $$ The trial-price slider lets you explore whether the market has excess demand or excess supply at prices other than \(p^*\).
