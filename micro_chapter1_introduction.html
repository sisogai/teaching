1.1-1.3 Economics and Rationality

Microeconomics studies how agents make choices under constraints and how those choices shape social outcomes. The rational-choice framework is used here as a disciplined benchmark: agents try to get the most from what is feasible for them.

Core idea

Mathematics is used to keep concepts such as feasibility, trade-offs, and optimality precise. The goal is not to replace verbal reasoning, but to make the logic checkable.

1.4 Functions, Graphs, and Differentiation

A function describes how one variable changes with another. For example, \(f(x)=140x\) converts US dollars into yen when the exchange rate is 140 yen per dollar.

Differentiation measures the local slope of a function. In economics, the same idea appears as marginal cost, marginal revenue, marginal utility, and marginal willingness to pay.
