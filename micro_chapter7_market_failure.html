7.1 Externality

An externality occurs when an economic activity affects other agents' welfare without passing through the market mechanism. The key distinction is between private marginal cost and social marginal cost.

Model: Production Externality

Inverse demand: \(P_D(Q)=a-bQ\). Private marginal cost: \(PMC(Q)=c+dQ\). If each unit of output creates a constant marginal external effect \(m\), the marginal social cost is: $$ MSC(Q) = PMC(Q) + m = c + m + dQ. $$ The private equilibrium sets \(P_D = PMC\): \(Q_{\text{private}} = \frac{a-c}{b+d}\). The social optimum sets \(P_D = MSC\): \(Q_{\text{social}} = \frac{a-c-m}{b+d}\). A Pigouvian tax equal to \(m\) per unit restores the social optimum; when \(m<0\), this is a subsidy for a positive externality.

7.2 Public Goods

Public goods are non-rival (one person's consumption does not reduce another's) and non-excludable (it is not possible to prevent anyone from consuming them). Because each consumer's benefit is independent, the efficient provision rule vertically sums individual marginal values and equates the total to marginal cost.

Samuelson Rule

The socially efficient quantity \(Q^*\) satisfies: $$ V_1(Q^*) + V_2(Q^*) = MC, $$ where \(V_i(Q)\) is consumer \(i\)'s marginal value of the public good. Unlike a private good, the efficient rule adds (vertically sums) individual marginal values rather than individual quantities. Free riding occurs when consumers underreport their valuations, shifting the reported social marginal value curve down and causing underprovision.
